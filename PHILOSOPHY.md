# Why This Archive Exists

Most problem collections optimize for coverage. This one optimizes for **transfer**.

Every problem here was chosen because it teaches a mental move — not a trick, but a *category of approach* that appears again and again in Putnam, in quant interviews, and in real probabilistic reasoning.

## The two questions for every problem

Before a problem enters this archive, it has to answer two questions clearly:

1. **What is the trap?** If there's no natural wrong path, the problem isn't teaching anything about how thinking fails.
2. **What does this train?** The solution is the proof. The insight is the point.

## On difficulty ratings

- **P1** — One key insight, clean algebra. A strong AMC student should get there in 10 minutes.
- **P2** — Requires combining two moves, or the setup is non-obvious. Putnam B1/A1 range.
- **P3** — The insight is genuinely hard to find. Putnam A3-A5 territory, or a hard quant interview problem.

A P1 problem with excellent editorial is more valuable than a P3 with a bare solution.

## On sources

Problems are drawn from: Putnam, AMC/AIME, classic probability texts (Feller, Ross), Jane Street puzzles, and original constructions. Source is always cited.
