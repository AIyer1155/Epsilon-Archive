ABOUT

The archive that teaches you how you think.

THE PROBLEM

Most problem sets are wrong about what they're teaching.

They present a problem, offer a solution, and call it preparation. At best, you learn that approach for that problem. But quant interviews and the Putnam don't test whether you've seen the trick before. They test whether you can generate the insight from scratch, under pressure, when everything in the problem is designed to point you somewhere else first.

"The answer is the least interesting part of any hard problem."

The cognitive mistake most students make isn't ignorance: it's pattern-matching to the wrong thing. A probability problem looks like a counting problem. A linear algebra question disguises itself as arithmetic. You reach for the tool that got used last time, and it fails. No one told you why, or what to watch for next time.

That's the gap Epsilon Archive is built to fill.

THE PHILOSOPHY

Every problem in this archive is treated as a case study in mathematical thinking, not just a vehicle for a technique.

The editorial format- Trap, Insight, Solution, What This Trains-exists because those four things are what a good mentor would tell you after you got a problem wrong. The trap names the mistake the problem is engineered to elicit. The insight is the reframe that dissolves the difficulty. The solution is rigorous but readable. And "What This Trains" connects the problem to the broader cognitive skill it belongs to.

This last piece is the most important. A student who understands what mental motion unlocked a problem can apply it the next time they see a completely different problem with the same underlying structure. That's what preparation actually means.

The archive is curated, not comprehensive. A smaller set of deeply understood problems beats a large set of half-understood ones, every time.

WHO IT'S FOR

Epsilon Archive is for three kinds of people, and it's honest about that.

AUDIENCE 01

Quant Candidates

Preparing for Jane Street, Citadel, Two Sigma, and similar. Looking for problems that reflect how top firms actually think, not textbook exercises.

AUDIENCE 02

Putnam Students

Aiming for real scores, not just participation. Want to understand problem structure and develop genuine mathematical taste, not just technique recall.

AUDIENCE 03

Math Educators

Interested in the editorial model as a teaching tool. The Trap/Insight framework is designed to be reusable in any context where understanding matters.
