STRUCTURED STUDY PATH

Eight weeks.
One framework.

A sequenced curriculum built for students starting from calculus and linear algebra, targeting Putnam competence and quant interview readiness by the end of week eight.

01
WEEK ONE

FOUNDATION

Mathematical Maturity and Problem Framing

Before diving into content, we establish the meta-skill: how to read a problem correctly. This week focuses on understanding what a problem is actually asking versus what it appears to be asking. We introduce the Trap/Insight framework and apply it to accessible problems across all seven categories.

PROBLEMS 001–002 FRAMEWORK INTRO PROOF WRITING BASICS
02
WEEK TWO

PROBABILITY I

Conditioning and Expectation

The single most important topic for quant interviews. We build from first principles: sample spaces, conditional probability, Bayes' theorem, and the linearity of expectation. Heavy emphasis on recognizing when linearity applies — it's almost always the key.

PROBLEMS 004, 010 ROSS CH. 1–2
03
WEEK THREE

COMBINATORICS

Counting Without Over-Counting

Bijections, inclusion-exclusion, generating functions, and the stars-and-bars technique. The emphasis here is on identifying the right counting method before starting — most mistakes in combinatorics come from counting the wrong thing, not computing incorrectly.

PROBLEMS 002, 009 PUTNAM COMBINATORICS SET
04
WEEK FOUR

LINEAR ALGEBRA

The Four Fundamental Subspaces

Rank, nullity, eigenvalues, and the geometric interpretation of linear maps. We move quickly past computation and toward understanding — what does it mean for a matrix to be singular, and what does an eigenvector actually represent?

PROBLEMS 003, 011 3B1B LINEAR ALGEBRA
05
WEEK FIVE

PROBABILITY II

Distributions, Convergence, and CLT

Common distributions and when to reach for each. The central limit theorem and its convergence properties. We revisit a key finding: convergence rate depends on the skewness of the underlying distribution — a subtlety most textbooks skip over.

PROBLEM 001 ROSS CH. 3–5 CLT CONVERGENCE STUDY
06
WEEK SIX

REAL ANALYSIS + NUMBER THEORY

Limits, Proofs, and Structure

Epsilon-delta definitions, convergence, and fixed-point theorems from analysis. Modular arithmetic and the structural approach to number theory problems. Both are Putnam favorites and neither requires more than clean reasoning to access.

PROBLEMS 005, 007, 013 PUTNAM ANALYSIS SET
07
WEEK SEVEN

GAME THEORY + ESTIMATION

Adversarial Thinking and Order-of-Magnitude Reasoning

Game theory as it appears in quant interviews: auctions, Nash equilibria, and optimal stopping. Paired with Fermi estimation — a distinct and trainable skill. The combination targets the two problem types most students are least prepared for.

PROBLEMS 006, 008, 012 JANE STREET PROBLEM ARCHIVE
08
WEEK EIGHT

INTEGRATION + REVIEW

Timed Practice and Gap Analysis

Full timed problem sets across all seven categories. The goal is not speed — it's identifying which Traps you still fall for, and which Insights you still need to be shown. Week eight is a diagnostic as much as it is a sprint. By the end, you know exactly what to work on next.
